How the Ledger rates programs

Each program receives one rating on each of three scales, taken from the program's statute, regulations, award terms, funding notices, guidance, official data catalogs and evaluations. Every rating cites the document and section it rests on, with the date it was read. The full rules are in the coding protocol and every column is defined in the codebook.

Required verification (R)

What recipients must report about results. This rates the obligation, not what happens in practice.

0Financial only
1Outputs
2Estimated outcomes
3Measured outcomes against a baseline
4Independent verification

Public results (C)

What anyone can see. Award announcements and recipient lists are not results.

0No public results
1Aggregate only
2Project-level outputs
3Project-level outcomes
4Open machine-readable project data

Attributability (A)

Whether the required or published data could let an outside party link outcomes to the funding.

0No baseline
1Baseline or modeled counterfactual only
2Measured before and after
3Comparison group or impact evaluation

What the Ledger cannot tell you

  • Public results measure publication, not collection. Agencies may hold outcome data they do not release.
  • Version 0.1.0 covers 24 programs chosen to span agencies, funding types and assets. It is not a complete or random sample.
  • The scales are ordinal. A gap of 2 is not twice a gap of 1.
  • The Ledger rates program design, not whether any project saved energy.

The full list is in LIMITATIONS.md.
