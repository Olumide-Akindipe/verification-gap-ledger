## 60 seconds

Cities and towns get billions in federal and state money for energy and infrastructure upgrades, and every program promises savings. But programs differ a lot in what proof they ask for. Some want receipts. Some want estimates. Very few want metered results against a baseline, checked by someone independent.

I built the Verification Gap Ledger to record that, program by program. It is an open dataset. For each program it rates what verification the rules require, what results are actually published, and whether anyone could tell the money caused the result. Every rating links to the exact rule or document.

The first version covers 24 federal and New York programs. Nearly half, 11 of 24, require no more than counts of what was installed. Only five require measured or independently verified results, and none of those five publish project-level results. Where independent measurement has been done, it matters: one New York evaluation found only about a fifth of estimated electricity savings in the utility bills.

It is free to use, citable with a DOI, and updated in versions through 2027.

## Two sentences

The Verification Gap Ledger is an open dataset that rates federal and New York municipal energy programs on what proof of results they require, what they publish, and whether outcomes can be attributed to the funding. Version 0.1 shows that 19 of 24 programs rely on outputs, estimates, or financial reports alone, and that none of the programs requiring measured results publish them at the project level.

## Likely questions

**Is this a sample of all programs?** No. Version 0.1 is 24 programs chosen to cover different agencies and funding types. Later versions widen the coverage.

**Couldn't agencies have the data and just not publish it?** Yes. The Ledger rates what is public, and says so. That is still what a municipality, auditor, or researcher can actually use.

**Does a low rating mean a program does not work?** No. It means the evidence to show whether it works is not required or not public.
